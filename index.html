01 / Overview

Everything the band needs.
Nothing it doesn’t.

01

One shared show state

Keep the band aligned around what is playing now and what comes next, whether performers join from a native Apple client or a browser.

NOWNeon WeatherShared across the band

A shared queue built for change

Search Songs or Setlists beside the live Queue, then add, reorder, or remove what comes next without hiding transport controls. Concurrent edits are applied against authoritative state while the current performance continues uninterrupted.

Private space to prepare

High-contrast Preview makes private inspection unmistakable and puts Add Next or Add to Queue beside the Song. Chart appearance, Display Key, instrument, spelling, and capo remain local to the performer and device.

“Built around the way a real stage actually works.”

02 / Design philosophy

Calm under
stage pressure.

01

Local first

The show should not depend on a distant service. Performance data stays close, responsive, and under the band’s control.

02

Clear boundaries

Shared playback, Queue, and Performance Key remain authoritative for the Band. Preview, workspace, and Display Key remain personal to each performer and device.

03

Performance focused

High-frequency actions stay visible, confirm success, and recover cleanly. Large targets, readable charts, and quiet reconnection help musicians keep moving under stage pressure.

04

Band shaped

Songs, members, libraries, and setlists reflect familiar band workflows instead of forcing the stage into office software.

05 / Current status

Active development

Tested on stage,
still earning trust.

LiveStage Pro has now run through full stage-network rehearsals and multiple live-show dogfooding sessions. The native macOS Host has synchronized iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, and browser performers while driving the existing external MIDI playback and lighting workflow.

Recent stage use produced a side-by-side Library and Queue workflow, clearer private Preview actions, consistent Play/Stop/Next behavior across clients, and an explicit separation between the Band’s Performance Key and each musician’s local Display Key. Re-cue recovery, adaptable charts, graceful reconnection, and local session diagnostics continue to support trust without hiding that the project remains an active, unreleased prototype.
