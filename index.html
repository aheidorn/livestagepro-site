01 / Overview

Everything the band needs.
Nothing it doesn’t.

01

One shared show state

Keep the band aligned around what is playing now and what comes next, whether performers join from a native Apple client or a browser.

NOWNeon WeatherShared across the band

A shared queue built for change

Search Songs or Setlists beside the live Queue, then add, reorder, remove, or move any waiting Song to Next without hiding transport controls. Concurrent requests are serialized against authoritative state while the current performance continues uninterrupted.

Private space to prepare

High-contrast Preview makes private inspection unmistakable and puts Add Next or Add to Queue beside the Song. Chart appearance, Display Key, instrument, spelling, and capo remain local to the performer and device.

“Make it go. Make it dependable. Then earn the next layer.”

02 / Design philosophy

Calm under
stage pressure.

01

Local first

The show should not depend on a distant service. Performance data stays close, responsive, and under the band’s control.

02

Clear boundaries

Shared playback, Queue, and Performance Key remain authoritative for the Band. Preview, workspace, and Display Key remain personal to each performer and device.

03

Performance focused

High-frequency actions stay visible, confirm success, and recover cleanly. Large targets, readable charts, and quiet reconnection help musicians keep moving under stage pressure.

04

Earn the next layer

An experiment can succeed without becoming a feature. Audio, MIDI, lighting, and automation expand only when real hardware, rehearsals, and performances show that they make the system more dependable.

03 / Development journey

Built one reliable
layer at a time.

LiveStage Pro is moving from a working prototype toward a trusted show system through small, testable milestones.

Foundation

Local performance model

Established the core model for bands, songs, members, libraries, and setlists.

Connection

Shared live sessions

Connected Mac and browser clients around authoritative show state, with clear separation between shared performance and private preparation.

Native

iPhone, iPad, and Watch

Added native performer workspaces, local session discovery, queue and setlist tools, private Preview, and a compact Watch transport companion.

On stage

From demonstration to dogfooding

Repeated rehearsals and live shows now drive the design: safer Queue editing, unmistakable Preview, consistent transport controls, adaptable charts, quieter reconnection, and explicit recovery tools.

Field proof

Two shows, one dependable system

LiveStage Pro performed successfully at Sovereign Estates Winery Chilifest and the Halloween Eve show, including recovery after a clean reinstall of the show computer’s operating system and software. With the hardened Mac workflow proven under real pressure, development has moved into an iPad Host feasibility prototype that adopts production Host behavior one bounded section at a time.

05 / Current status

Active development

Proven through two gigs,
building the next Host.

The hardened Mac system performed wonderfully at both Sovereign Estates Winery Chilifest and the Halloween Eve show. Songs, Queue changes, external playback, MIDI, lighting, native Apple clients, and browser performers worked together through real set changes—and the production workflow recovered even after the show computer required a clean reinstall of its operating system and software.

Apple Watch control worked well for 37 Songs, with brief reconnection trouble during a couple of others. The paired iPhone sleeping may be involved, but the cause is not yet established. New bounded phone-and-Watch diagnostics can collect untethered lifecycle, link, snapshot, and command evidence for correlation with the Host performance log, allowing the investigation to proceed without weakening the system’s freshness and fail-safe rules.

Development has now started on a separate iPad Host M1 prototype. It embeds the same authoritative server runtime, imports complete Band archives into its own container, remembers the selected Band, serves the existing WebClient, advertises active Sessions through Bonjour, and exposes native Play/Pause, Stop, Next, and Re-cue controls. Preview, Queue, and transport authority still follow the same boundaries as the Mac system.

The prototype has also reached the real performance path: Song MIDI Actions and audio-synchronized companion MIDI can use an explicitly selected iPad MIDI destination, with initial RTP-MIDI routing to the lighting rig confirmed. Audio-session safety stops on real interruptions or output loss, while active backing audio can continue during app switching or screen lock. Background hosting between Songs, Global Action orchestration, editing, export, and broader recovery UX remain unfinished.

LiveStage Pro remains a functioning prototype. The Mac is still the complete production Host; the iPad work is a feasibility build, not a gig-ready replacement. Each next slice must earn physical acceptance with real Band content, audio, MIDI, lighting, native and browser clients, interruption handling, and sustained background behavior before iPad hosting—or later router-free networking—is presented as supported.
